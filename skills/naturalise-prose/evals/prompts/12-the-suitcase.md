Mrs Bell put the suitcase on the counter with its broken handle facing Felix.

"You said the handle."

"Yeah. That's the main thing."

She opened the case. The zip stopped halfway round.

"And this?"

"It does that. You have to sort of—"

"I know what a zip does."

Felix took his hands out of his pockets and then, having no other use for them, returned them to the same location. His father had told him to get the case back by four. The train left at five, and he would rather travel without a shirt, he had said, than miss it. Felix had not asked why this had been presented as the only alternative.

"I can replace the handle today," Mrs Bell said. "The zip will take longer."

"How much longer are we talking? Because he sort of needs it. Today."

She made a small movement of her head in the direction of the clock.

"Then he should have brought it yesterday."

Felix rubbed a thumb over the counter's worn edge. "He wasn't going yesterday."

Mrs Bell let go of the zip. She looked at him for a moment before beginning to look at the case again.

"Handle by four," she said. "Tell him to put a belt round the rest."

"He's only got the one."

"Then he'd better keep his trousers on."
