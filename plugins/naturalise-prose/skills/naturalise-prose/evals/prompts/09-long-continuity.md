## Purpose and scope

The council's Quiet Window trial allowed participating shops on Market Street to receive weekday deliveries between 09:00 and 10:30. The first observation period lasted six weeks. Eighteen of the 22 invited shops took part; this report calls those 18 shops the Trial Group. The other four shops continued with their existing arrangements.

The trial was intended to reduce deliveries during the busiest part of the school journey. It did not cover collections from shops, weekend deliveries, or streets elsewhere in the town. These limits matter when interpreting the results: an improvement on Market Street would not, by itself, establish what would happen on another street.

## What the records show

Fifteen shops in the Trial Group used the Quiet Window at least once. The remaining three did not use it during the observation period. Participation and use are therefore different measures, and neither tells us whether a shop wants the arrangement to continue.

The council logged nine formal complaints about deliveries on Market Street in the first fortnight and four in the final fortnight. These figures count complaints, not delivery vehicles or individual residents. A single resident could have made more than one complaint. The middle fortnight is not included in this comparison.

It is important to note that roadworks began nearby during the second week. Delivery volumes also changed, but the council did not collect reliable counts. Officers could not separate the effect of the Quiet Window from these other changes. The reduction in complaints is an observation, not a finding that the trial caused the reduction.

## Feedback

Twelve shops in the Trial Group returned the questionnaire. Eight preferred to keep the Quiet Window, three wanted its hours changed, and one was undecided. The six shops that did not respond cannot be counted as supporting or opposing an extension.

The three requests to change the hours did not agree on a replacement slot. Two asked for a later start; the third asked for an earlier finish. The questionnaire did not ask respondents to choose between those alternatives, so it provides no basis for describing either as the preferred replacement.

Several residents separately asked the council to keep the pavement clear during deliveries. Their comments concerned access past parked vehicles. They were not responses to the shop questionnaire and should not be combined with its totals.

## Conditions for an extension

The council is considering a loading bay on Mill Lane as part of a possible extension. An accessibility officer must approve the bay's layout before the committee can consider extending the trial. That approval would cover the proposed bay, not the delivery hours or a permanent scheme.

The bay would not be available for deliveries under the current permission. Until a new permission is issued, the Trial Group must use the existing loading arrangements. Asking for the layout to be assessed does not authorize shops to start using the proposed bay.

## What happens next

The committee is due to review the layout proposal on 14 June. No extension has been approved. The existing trial permission remains in force until 28 June and applies only to the Trial Group.

In practical terms, this means participating shops can continue under the current conditions until that date. Other shops cannot join on the strength of this report. Any decision about an extension will need to state who may take part, which delivery hours apply, and when the new permission begins.
